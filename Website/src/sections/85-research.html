Marswalk: Earth-Independent Planning and Guidance for Walking EVAs on Mars

Kumar Sashank Ghanta, Ivan Chen, George Postica, Mirim Yu, Michelle Wang · Team NASASpaceHacks, Toronto

Earth can't help a Mars crew in real time, and the planners that already run on Mars are built for rovers, not for a suited human's oxygen, heat and walk home. The paper presents Marswalk: a habitat planner that checks every walk against twelve sourced EVA constraints, re-checks it in a separate simulator and explains its verdict, then hands a signed route of about 11 kB to a phone AR navigator that keeps guiding when the link drops. It covers the engine, the GPS-free navigation design, a suit hardware concept and an evaluation against measured NASA data.

Proposed architecture
  1. Habitat workstation prepares the routeA terrain-aware route, with waypoints, route version, coordinate frame, hazard flags and integrity fields.
  2. A low-data route packet is cached in the suitStored before EVA, so a lost radio link cannot interrupt local navigation.
  3. Boot-mounted inertial sensors, plus camera imagery, give local pose6-axis IMUs for pedestrian dead reckoning with zero-velocity updates, and helmet/torso camera visual odometry. Intermittent orbital-map matching may add absolute corrections. No reliance on a magnetic compass.
  4. A monocular optical see-through display guides the walkNext direction, distance and navigation confidence, including an explicit degraded-confidence state.
  5. Telemetry to the habitat while in coverage; local guidance when the link dropsThe habitat supervises but is not in the control loop. After a link loss, freshness and route-version checks run before new instructions are accepted.
Built on NASA’s published pilot, not our result

NASA Johnson Space Center Joint AR pilot: 5 astronaut-office participants, AR navigation vs paper map, Earth field analog.

Median navigation time15.87 → 13.20 min

Paper map → Joint AR. A small pilot in an Earth analog, measured with field GPS: it motivates testing the design hypothesis and does not validate Marswalk or show any improvement on Mars. Reference [A1] in the study.

Principal risks the study names
  • Helmet optical fit: eye relief, moving eyebox, glare
  • Yaw and camera pose uncertainty: a misaligned arrow can be worse than none
  • Poor orbital-map match causing a false position reset
  • Power, thermal and radiation effects on compute and display
  • Terrain blocking habitat radio; false or stale route updates
Proposed validation progression
  • Replay sensor and camera data through a software pipeline
  • Ground-based Mars-analog route with surveyed checkpoints
  • Worst-case reconnection and route-revision tests
  • Measure display readability with representative helmet geometry
  • Derive a qualified power, reliability and environmental test plan
No tests are claimed as complete.
